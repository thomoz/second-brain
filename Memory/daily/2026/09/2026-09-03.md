**[WhatsApp]** 61410868612@c.us: Which way will 10 year BONDS go up or down if interest rates rise when the fed makes their decision?
**[Brain]** Down in price.

Key point:
- If interest rates rise, existing 10-year fixed-rate bonds usually fall in price.
- Their yield moves up as the price falls.

Important nuance:
- The Fed controls the short-term policy rate, not the 10-year directly.
- So on decision day, the 10-year only falls if the market pushes 10-year yields higher.
- If the hike was already priced in, or the Fed sounds dovish, 10-year bonds can rise instead.

Authoritative reference: the SEC’s Investor.gov says bond prices and market interest rates generally move in opposite directions: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-86

**[Brain]** Kestrel note: yes, broadly lower rates make US debt easier to carry.

Lower rates help because:
The government pays less interest when new debt is issued or old debt is rolled over.
That reduces budget pressure over time.
It does not erase the debt, but it slows how fast interest costs blow out.

But the Fed is not supposed to cut rates just to help Treasury finance debt.
Its main priorities are:
Inflation
Employment
Financial stability

So in practice:
If inflation is still a problem, rates can stay high even if that hurts the debt picture.
If the economy weakens and inflation cools, lower rates become easier to justify.

Lower debt servicing cost
Less stress on housing and business borrowing
Less recession risk

But dealing with US debt is usually not the top short-term priority.
Short-term priority is usually:
Keep inflation under control
Avoid a bad recession
Keep unemployment from spiking

US debt is more of a medium-to-long-term structural problem.
Inflation and the job market are the day-to-day emergency dashboard.

Simple version:
Lower rates help the debt.
But rates are mainly set based on inflation and employment, not as a debt-relief tool.
