**[Brain]** Likely downward pressure on the US dollar, but not automatically a crash.

If foreign central banks keep selling Treasuries, they usually receive US dollars and then may convert those dollars into euros, yen, gold, or other assets. That creates selling pressure on USD.

The bigger effect may be through bond yields: less foreign demand for Treasuries means the US may need to offer higher yields to attract buyers. Higher yields can sometimes support the dollar, because global investors chase returns. So the net result depends on which force dominates.

My read: steady foreign selling is bearish for long-term dollar dominance, especially if paired with US deficits, inflation risk, sanctions risk, or loss of confidence. But in a crisis, the dollar can still rise because it remains the main reserve and safe-haven currency.

Key point: falling foreign Treasury ownership weakens the dollar’s structural support, but the dollar only falls hard if private/global investors also stop wanting USD assets.
