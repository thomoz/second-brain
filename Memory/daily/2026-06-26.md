For your VPS, there are really 2 separate jobs:

2. recognise that the speaker is you

Voice to text:
- very doable

- good for WhatsApp voice notes, memos, and quick idea capture

Recognising it’s you:
- also possible, but separate
- that needs speaker verification, not just transcription
- useful if multiple people might send audio and you only want yours treated specially

Main tradeoffs:
- local on VPS = better privacy, no per-minute API fee, but more CPU/RAM load
- cloud API = easier and often faster, but ongoing cost and less privacy
- accuracy depends on audio quality, background noise, and your accent/speaking speed

For your setup, the practical version is:
- upload voice note
- transcribe on VPS
- optionally check “is this Shaun?”
- save text into Memory or route it into your WhatsApp workflow

Best first version:
- do transcription first
- skip speaker recognition unless you actually need to distinguish your voice from others

**[Brain]** Yes, that’s the sensible way.

Metadata you might still want:

- better privacy
- less clutter
- simpler to manage on the VPS

Only reason to keep audio:
- transcription failed
- you want to reprocess later with a better model
- you need an audit trail
- you want speaker recognition later

Practical compromise:
- delete audio by default after successful transcription
- keep failed or low-confidence files for, say, 7 days
- then auto-delete those too

That would be the cleanest design for your use case.
