Methodology & assumptions

How the test was run — signals, execution, costs, and the one judgment call

Signal & rule

Every day, the S&P 500 index (^GSPC) close is compared to its own simple moving average. Long requires close above the average and the average itself higher than the prior day's average (rising). Bearish requires close below the average and the average lower than the prior day's (falling). No 5-day confirmation, no percentage buffer. When price and slope disagree, the system holds its existing position — it only flips once the opposite condition is fully satisfied.

Execution & look-ahead

Signals are computed on ^GSPC's close. The trade itself executes on SPY at the next trading day's open — never the same close that generated the signal. On a transition day the return is split into an overnight leg (old position, prior close → today's open) and an intraday leg (new position, today's open → close), so the switch costs exactly what it would have in real time.

Dividends, counted once

Returns are built from SPY's raw Open/Close plus its actual historical dividend payments — never from a pre-adjusted "Adj Close" series. That keeps dividends from being counted twice, and lets a short position correctly pay the dividend (economically identical to inverting the instrument's whole total return, price plus income) rather than just inverting the price change.

Initial position, 4 Jan 1999

At every moving-average length tested, the S&P 500 on the first trading day of 1999 was already above a rising average — the long condition was outright satisfied, so both the Long/Cash and Long/Short systems simply start long on day one. No tiebreak or discretionary call was needed at any length.

Costs & financing — before vs. after

"Before costs" means zero commission, slippage, and borrow fee. "After costs" applies all of the below. The chosen cash-interest treatment (0% vs. T-bill) is a strategy variant, not a cost, so it applies identically in both views.

Commission
$3.00 / leg (≈$6 round trip — matches IBKR-style per-trade cost on a long)
Slippage
2 bps of notional / leg
Short borrow fee
0.30%/yr on notional while short (SPY: easy-to-borrow)
Cash interest
Actual ^IRX 13-week T-bill yield, accrued daily (Long/Cash T-bill variant only)
Signal instrument
^GSPC close vs. SMA
Execution instrument
SPY raw Open/Close + Dividends

Real-world caveats this doesn't model: IBKR's actual per-share commission schedule (the flat $3 above is a simplifying stand-in you can retune), margin-interest spreads beyond the stated borrow fee, and any AUD/USD conversion drag on an Australian account trading a USD-denominated ETF.
