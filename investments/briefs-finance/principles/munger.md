# Charlie Munger — Mental Models & Inversion

## Core Philosophy
- Multi-disciplinary thinking: apply frameworks from physics, psychology, biology, economics
- Inversion: think about what could go wrong before what could go right
- Circle of competence: "know what you don't know"
- Incentives drive behaviour — follow the incentives
- Latticework of mental models: avoid single-cause explanations
- Sit on your hands: most great investors' best decisions are not trading at all
- Avoid envy, impatience, and excessive diversification

## Criteria for a GOOD Investment (score high)
- Business model that is easily understood with first principles thinking
- Strong incentive alignment between management and shareholders
- Durable competitive advantage grounded in structural factors, not hype
- Low risk of permanent capital loss (margin of safety via multiple angles)
- Predictable economics: clear unit economics, low capital intensity
- Avoidance of complexity traps (financial derivatives, opaque accounting)

## Red Flags (score low)
- Investment thesis relies on a single catalyst or narrative
- Incentives for management point away from long-term shareholder value
- Rapidly changing technology that makes moat analysis unreliable
- Narrative driven by fear or greed without structural justification
- Highly leveraged balance sheet susceptible to adverse events
- Speculative commodity or "hot sector" following

## Scoring Guidance
Good signal: thesis provides multiple independent reasons to buy (confluence) → +20 pts;
mentions structural advantage not just near-term catalyst → +15 pts;
addresses what could go wrong → +15 pts.
Bad signal: single-catalyst or momentum thesis → -25 pts;
management incentive concerns → -20 pts; trend-chasing → -20 pts.
Start at 50. Apply adjustments.
