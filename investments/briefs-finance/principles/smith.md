# Terry Smith — Quality at a Reasonable Price

## Core Philosophy
- Buy good companies. Don't overpay. Do nothing (avoid unnecessary trading).
- Three criteria: (1) high-quality business, (2) don't overpay, (3) leave it alone
- Quality = high and sustainable return on capital (ROC/ROCE ≥ 15%)
- Avoid companies that grow through acquisitions or financial engineering
- The best companies are self-funding — they don't need debt or equity issuance to grow
- Operating cash flow should match or exceed reported earnings (accounting quality)

## Criteria for a GOOD Investment (score high)
- Return on capital employed (ROCE) consistently above 15%
- Business generates more cash than it consumes (self-funding)
- Strong intangible assets: brands, patents, market position
- Gross margins consistently high (>50% for software/brands, >30% for others)
- Revenue growth funded by operations, not leverage or equity raises
- Pricing power demonstrated over multiple economic cycles
- Accounting is conservative (no unusual off-balance-sheet items)

## Red Flags (score low)
- Low return on capital or declining trend
- Acquisition-led growth strategy (destroys long-term value)
- Company requires ongoing debt or equity issuance to fund operations
- Reported earnings consistently exceed cash flow (accounting red flag)
- Commodity business with no differentiation
- Recent PE-backed IPO or spin-off (often over-levered)
- Cyclical business masquerading as a growth stock

## Scoring Guidance
Thesis mentions: high ROCE or margins → +20 pts; self-funding / no dilution → +15 pts;
strong intangible assets or brand → +15 pts; conservative accounting → +10 pts.
Deduct: acquisition strategy → -20 pts; low margins/ROCE → -20 pts;
cash burn or equity raises → -25 pts.
Start at 50. Apply adjustments.
