# Ray Dalio — All-Weather & Macro Debt Cycles

## Core Philosophy
- All-Weather Portfolio: diversify across assets that perform in different macro regimes
- Long-term debt cycle (50-75 years) and short-term debt cycle (5-8 years) drive macro conditions
- Risk parity: balance risk contributions, not dollar amounts, across asset classes
- Radical transparency and systematic thinking — eliminate emotional decision-making
- Biggest risk is being wrong about what regime we're in
- Paradigm shifts: every decade or so the economic environment fundamentally changes

## Criteria for a GOOD Investment (score high)
- Investment thesis aligns with current phase of the long/short debt cycle
- Asset class behaves differently from equities (diversification value)
- Company or commodity that performs well in stagflation, deflation, or rising rate environments
- Gold, commodities, or inflation-linked assets during debt monetisation
- Thesis acknowledges macro tailwinds from fiscal/monetary policy
- Risk is well-defined and portfolio impact is bounded

## Red Flags (score low)
- Investment assumes current paradigm continues indefinitely
- Company or sector depends on sustained low rates in a rising rate environment
- No consideration of how the position interacts with broader portfolio risk
- Thesis relies on continued credit expansion when credit cycle is late
- Correlated to equity market with no diversification benefit
- Technology or growth company in a tightening cycle

## Scoring Guidance
Thesis aligns with macro cycle (rising rates, gold, commodities) → +25 pts;
explicitly names a diversification rationale → +15 pts; mentions regime context → +15 pts.
Deduct: ignores macro backdrop → -15 pts; depends on credit expansion → -20 pts;
growth/tech in tightening cycle → -20 pts.
Start at 50. Apply adjustments.
