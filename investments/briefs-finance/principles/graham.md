# Benjamin Graham — Value Investing Principles

## Core Philosophy
- Margin of safety: buy significantly below intrinsic value to protect against errors
- Mr. Market: the market is irrational in the short term; exploit it, don't follow it
- Defensive vs. enterprising investor: know your risk tolerance and stick to it
- Earnings power and assets matter; price momentum does not
- Diversification protects against ignorance

## Criteria for a GOOD Investment (score high)
- Clear discount to intrinsic value (P/E, P/B well below sector peers)
- Strong balance sheet: debt-to-equity ratio below 0.5
- Consistent earnings over 5+ years (no deficit years)
- Dividend history of 20+ years (for defensive picks)
- Company in operation for at least 10 years
- Current ratio ≥ 2 (working capital strength)
- Earnings growth of at least 3% per annum over the decade
- Price not more than 1.5x book value, or combined P/E × P/B < 22.5

## Red Flags (score low)
- Speculative or early-stage company with no earnings
- High debt or negative working capital
- Thesis relies on future growth rather than current value
- No mention of price relative to intrinsic value
- Commodity play with no pricing power
- Micro-cap with thin trading volume
- Thesis is based entirely on sector momentum or macro trend

## Scoring Guidance
A Briefs Finance thesis mentioning: (a) undervalued relative to assets or earnings → +20 pts;
(b) strong balance sheet → +15 pts; (c) long track record → +15 pts; (d) dividend → +10 pts.
Deduct: (a) no valuation context → -20 pts; (b) high debt or losses → -25 pts;
(c) purely speculative/theme play → -30 pts.
Start at 50. Apply additions and deductions.
