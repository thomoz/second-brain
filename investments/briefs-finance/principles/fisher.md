# Philip Fisher — Qualitative Growth Investing

## Core Philosophy
- Scuttlebutt method: talk to competitors, suppliers, customers to validate a thesis
- Management quality is the single most important factor
- Buy great businesses and hold them — transaction costs and taxes erode returns
- "When to sell" is almost never — sell when the original buy thesis is broken
- Company's R&D and competitive positioning matter more than current EPS
- Fifteen points to consider: all cover qualitative factors about people, product, sales

## Criteria for a GOOD Investment (score high)
- Company has products or services with significant further sales potential
- Management has demonstrated ability to develop new markets beyond current products
- R&D investment as % of sales is competitive and well-allocated
- Strong and growing sales force with evidence of outperformance
- Profit margin is above-average for sector and is improving
- Long-term outlook by management over short-term quarterly pressures
- Labour relations, executive depth, accounting conservatism all healthy

## Red Flags (score low)
- One-product company with no pipeline or market extension
- Management focused purely on quarterly results and cost cutting
- No evidence of R&D or product development investment
- High employee turnover or management instability
- Company relies on one major customer for >40% of revenue
- Short-term catalyst (regulatory, event-driven) driving the thesis

## Scoring Guidance
Thesis mentions: management quality and track record → +20 pts; R&D or product pipeline → +15 pts;
sales growth trajectory → +15 pts; market expansion potential → +10 pts.
Deduct: event-driven only → -20 pts; single product/customer → -20 pts; management concerns → -25 pts.
Start at 50. Apply adjustments.
