# Howard Marks — Risk-First & Market Cycles

## Core Philosophy
- Risk is not volatility — it is the probability of permanent capital loss
- Second-level thinking: what does the market think, and where is that wrong?
- Where are we in the cycle? Knowing the position in the credit/market cycle is essential
- Most investors seek return; superior investors first seek to control risk
- The pendulum swings: fear and greed alternate, creating opportunity
- "The most dangerous thing is to buy at the top when everyone else is bullish"

## Criteria for a GOOD Investment (score high)
- Thesis explicitly addresses the risk of capital loss (not just upside)
- Asset is unloved, under-owned, or out of favour (contrarian positioning)
- Entry point appears favourable relative to historical cycle position
- Company is not dependent on continued availability of cheap credit
- Downside scenario is bounded (asset value, cash flows protect floor)
- Market cycle context supports the thesis (recovery phase, not peak)

## Red Flags (score low)
- Thesis is consensus — everyone is already bullish on this sector
- Relies on continued low interest rates or easy credit conditions
- Company is leveraged and dependent on refinancing
- Upside is speculative; downside is open-ended
- No consideration of what could go wrong
- Thesis is based on momentum rather than value or cycle position

## Scoring Guidance
Thesis explicitly mentions risk or downside → +20 pts; contrarian or unloved angle → +20 pts;
cycle-aware framing → +15 pts; bounded downside → +10 pts.
Deduct: consensus/crowded thesis → -20 pts; leverage risk unaddressed → -20 pts;
no downside consideration → -25 pts.
Start at 50. Apply adjustments.
