# Kerr Neilson — Platinum Asset Management Style

## Core Philosophy
- Contrarian global value investor — seek out-of-favour markets and sectors
- Superior returns come from buying what others are selling and selling what others are buying
- Long investment time horizon (3-5 years minimum)
- Emerging markets and overlooked geographies offer the best risk/reward when sentiment is low
- Beware consensus and recency bias — the most crowded trades carry the most risk
- Currency and macro tailwinds matter — especially for Australian investors buying global assets

## Criteria for a GOOD Investment (score high)
- Sector or geography is currently out of favour with institutional consensus
- Company trades at a significant discount to global peers for non-structural reasons
- Management has long tenure and a track record through multiple cycles
- Earnings are depressed temporarily (cyclical, not structural)
- Macro tailwind: currency, commodity cycle, or regulatory shift in company's favour
- Track record of capital allocation through downturns

## Red Flags (score low)
- Sector is currently popular or overowned by institutional investors
- Company is benefiting from a temporary regulatory or political windfall
- Thesis does not address why the discount exists and when it will close
- Emerging market exposure without currency hedging consideration
- No evidence management has navigated previous cycles
- Thesis assumes continued momentum rather than reversal

## Scoring Guidance
Thesis identifies a discount relative to peers for non-structural reasons → +25 pts;
mentions out-of-favour geography or sector → +20 pts; long-term cycle view → +15 pts;
currency or macro tailwind acknowledged → +10 pts.
Deduct: consensus/popular sector → -20 pts; momentum-only thesis → -20 pts;
structural discount (not temporary) → -25 pts.
Start at 50. Apply adjustments.
