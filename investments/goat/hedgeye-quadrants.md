# Hedgeye Growth/Inflation Quadrants — Reference

Research notes only — not a live tool, not wired into any scan. See
`investments/goat/growth-inflation-quadrant-handoff.md` for the fuller research
handoff (what experts say, backtest caveats, whether it's worth building).

## Who made it

**Hedgeye's "GIP Model" (Growth, Inflation, Policy)** — Keith McCullough,
founder/CEO of Hedgeye Risk Management. Canadian, Yale econ grad, was a hedge
fund PM (Carlyle Blue Wave, Magnetar, Dawson-Herman) before founding Hedgeye in
2008. His pitch: as a PM he found getting the macro call right mattered more
than individual stock-picking, so he built the quad framework as a systematic
way to make that call. The "Quad 1–4" labeling is his firm's branding
specifically.

**The actual origin of the idea** predates Hedgeye by four years: **Trevor
Greetham**, Merrill Lynch's Director of Asset Allocation, published "The
Investment Clock: Making Money from Macro" in 2004, backtested on 30+ years of
US data. Same two axes (growth vs. trend, inflation rising vs. falling), same
four regimes, just named differently (Reflation / Recovery / Overheat /
Stagflation instead of Quad 4/1/2/3). Hedgeye's version is a repackaging of an
established idea, not something McCullough invented from scratch.

## Sectors, industries, and example companies per quadrant

| Quad | Definition | Leading sectors | Avoid / underweight | Example companies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Growth ↑, Inflation ↓ ("Goldilocks") | Technology, Consumer Discretionary, Communication Services, Industrials | Utilities, Staples, Energy | Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta; Caterpillar, Deere (industrials) |
| **2** | Growth ↑, Inflation ↑ ("Overheat"/Reflation) | Energy, Materials, Financials (rising rates), Industrials | Long bonds, rate-sensitive REITs | Exxon, Chevron, ConocoPhillips; Freeport-McMoRan, Nucor; JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs |
| **3** | Growth ↓, Inflation ↑ (Stagflation) | Consumer Staples, Healthcare, Utilities, gold/real assets | Consumer Discretionary, small-caps, high-beta tech | Procter & Gamble, Colgate, Coca-Cola, Walmart, Costco; Johnson & Johnson, UnitedHealth; Newmont, Barrick Gold |
| **4** | Growth ↓, Inflation ↓ (Deflationary bust) | Healthcare, Staples, REITs, Utilities, long-duration Treasuries | Tech, Energy, Industrials, small-caps | J&J, UnitedHealth, Merck; P&G, Coca-Cola; Realty Income, NextEra Energy, Duke Energy |

Style-factor pattern is consistent across sources: Quad 1 rewards
Momentum/High-Beta/Growth stocks; Quad 4 rewards High-Dividend/Low-Beta/Quality
— the two are near-mirror opposites, which is the whole logic of "rotate"
rather than "stay put."

**Caveat from the research:** Quad 2 and Quad 3 sector lists vary more across
sources than Quad 1/Quad 4 do — the "goldilocks" and "bust" ends of the cycle
are well-agreed on, the middle two are messier in practice (matches Fidelity's
own finding that mid-cycle transitions produce the least sector
differentiation).
