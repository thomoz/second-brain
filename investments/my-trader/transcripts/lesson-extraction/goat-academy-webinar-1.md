## 3 things to do to improve investing decisions:

Step 1. Read the scoreboard:

Note: index funds of industries/sectors are a better bet than trying to pick individual stocks.

Which industries are making money and which are not? In a chart, look for a sideways, smooth heartbeat pattern (smooth up-down-up-down - 3 months minimum - the longer the better - all the 10x stocks had a heartbeat of 2-4 years before going up 10x) , then the easiest "buy" rule is when the heartbeat breaks above its 50 day moving average (a break out) and the 50 day moving average is now sloping up instead of down. Then, for risk mamangement, check the company's fundamentals (these are in order of importance): Debt (zero debt is great); Cash runway in years; Margin (is it ok?); Revenue Growth; Are they generating cash? What we are looking for is: how likely are they to survive in the near-term (how unlikely is it that they will go bankrupt)? If they are likely to survive, it could be a good buy.

Step 2. You Don't Have to Pick Stocks:

When a stock drops, it's not the wrong stock: you are buying the wrong industry. Industries (sectors of stocks) tend to rise and fall at the same time i.e. find the rising indutsry and invest in that (an index fund of it - not single stocks), and sell out of industries (indexes) that are beginning to show signs of falling.

So, what's the right industry? It's not about timing the market - it's about follow the institutional money.

Step 3. The Exit Matters:

The number 1 mistake that investors make is holding onto a winner until it becomes a loser. Easiest sell rule: when stop dips below 150 day moving average (reasonably below, as sometimes prices break through it slightly but come back up above), it's probably time to sell.
